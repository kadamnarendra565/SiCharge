EXPERIMENT NO: 04 DATE: 07 / 08 / 2026

Developing a Financial Plan & Break-even Analysis

Aim:

To help students understand startup costing, revenue estimation, and break-even analysis by preparing a basic financial plan for their business idea.

Objectives:

  • • Identify various fixed and variable costs in starting and operating.
  • • Estimate revenue based on pricing and sales projections.
  • • Perform break-even analysis using cost-revenue relationships.
  • • Interpret financial feasibility for a startup idea.
Theme / Startup Concept:

SiCharge — Silicon Carbide Power Module for EV & Industrial Batteries

SiCharge designs and assembles silicon-carbide (SiC) MOSFET-based power modules that replace conventional silicon modules inside EV and industrial battery packs, cutting energy losses and enabling faster charging (concept adapted from the SiC industry Business Model Canvas — customer segments: EV/motor-drive OEMs, aerospace electronics, renewable energy & data-centre power suppliers).

Step 1: Startup Cost Structure Cost Breakdown Table
Fixed Costs (One-Time / Capex):
  • • SiC module design & prototyping: ₹1,80,000
  • • Battery-pack integration engineering: ₹1,50,000
  • • Certification & reliability testing: ₹1,00,000
  • • Branding & promotion: ₹20,000
  • Total Fixed Costs: ₹4,50,000
Variable Costs (Per Unit):
  • • SiC MOSFET chips & substrate: ₹8,000
  • • Assembly, wiring & QC testing: ₹1,500
  • • Packaging & logistics: ₹500
  • Total Variable Cost per unit: ₹10,000
Step 2: Revenue Model
Selling price per SiC power module: ₹15,000 / unit
Expected pilot customers (Q1 orders): 50 units (EV OEM & Motor-drive pilots)
Step 3: Break-Even Analysis Equation & Solution
Break-even point (units) = Fixed Costs ÷ (Selling Price per Unit − Variable Cost per Unit)
= ₹4,50,000 ÷ (₹15,000 − ₹10,000)
= ₹4,50,000 ÷ ₹5,000 = 90 units

Conclusion: 90 units must be sold to completely cover all startup fixed expenditures.

Step 4: Profit Forecast & Feasibility Interpretation
Monthly profit at 150 units:
₹7,50,000 / month
(₹15,000 − ₹10,000) × 150 units
Calculated Payback Period:
~0.6 months (under 3 weeks)
After reaching 150-unit/month run-rate

Note: Figures above model an assembly & integration startup built on top of purchased SiC chips from established foundries. This validates high financial feasibility with minimal capex barriers.
